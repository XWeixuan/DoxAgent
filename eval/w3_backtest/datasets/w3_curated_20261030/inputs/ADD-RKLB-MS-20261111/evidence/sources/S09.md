# Rocket Lab Q3 earnings call

## Relevant excerpt

CFO, Rocket Lab
Thanks, Pete. Third quarter 2026 revenue was a record $267.8 million, coming in above our prior guidance range of $250 million-$265 million, and representing year-over-year growth of 73%. Revenue increased by 14.4% sequentially, driven by strong contribution from both business segments, highlighting continued momentum across the business. Our space systems segment delivered $214.8 million in revenue for the quarter, reflecting a sequential increase of 13.3%.
This growth was primarily driven by continued scaling of our satellite platform business, along with full quarter contributions from our laser communications and robotics acquisitions. We also delivered sequential organic growth across our other component and software businesses. Our space systems business continues to perform exceptionally well and provides diversification alongside our robust, but at times lumpy, launch business.
Meanwhile, our launch services segment generated $53.1 million in revenue, representing a 19% quarter-over-quarter increase, driven by a two-launch increase in point-in-time Electron missions, where the full value of the launch is reflected at launch. Now, turning to gross margin. GAAP gross margin for the third quarter was 33.6%, above our prior guidance range of 29%-31%. Non-GAAP gross margin for the third quarter was 38.4%, which was above our prior guidance range of 35%-37%.
Consistent with our Q3 guidance, margins declined sequentially, primarily driven by the first full quarter contribution from our recent acquisition, Mynaric, which brings laser communications capabilities to our portfolio, as well as the lack of tariff refunds that benefited Q2 margins.
The decline was smaller than we anticipated, however, due to upside in our component businesses' profitability, led by solar and our Geost acquisitions' optical systems, as well as launch favorability driven by reduced production spend and favorable absorption due to a step-up in Electron launch and build cadence. As previously mentioned, Mynaric will take a few quarters to reach target margins as we align the cost structure, scale production, and implement the Rocket Lab operating system.
Additionally, we recorded a forward loss reserve against our Neutron Flight 2 launch vehicle to reflect the nascent stage of the vehicle and related early launch capability and potential impacts to that specific flight's profitability, or lack thereof. Relatedly, we ended Q3 with production-related headcount of 1,728, up 40 from the prior quarter.
Turning to backlog, we ended Q3 2026 with approximately $3.6 billion in total backlog, up 52% sequentially from approximately $2.4 billion at the end of Q2, with launch backlog accounting for approximately 52% and space systems representing 48%. We signed a significant volume of contracts across launch and space systems in the third quarter, bringing our backlog to 117 launches and over 55 spacecraft, and making 2026 an incredible year of backlog growth thus far.
As Pete mentioned, we signed 54 launches in the quarter, eight of which were for Neutron, with multiple contracts at a new escalated base price of $70 million, underscoring the growing demand and scarcity for medium-lift launch capacity. While bookings across launch and space systems remain inherently lumpy due to the timing of larger, high-impact program opportunities, backlog has grown even as we recognize revenue at a meaningfully higher run rate.
We continue to pursue additional needle-moving contract opportunities that we expect will enable us to sustain healthy backlog levels as revenue continues to scale. Looking ahead, we expect approximately 32.9% of our current backlog to convert into revenue within the next 12 months. Additionally, we continue to benefit from relatively quick turns business across launch and space systems components businesses that drive incremental top-line contribution beyond the current 12-month backlog conversion.
