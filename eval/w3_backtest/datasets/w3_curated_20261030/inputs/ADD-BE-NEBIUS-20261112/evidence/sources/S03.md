# Vineland stop-work orders

Publisher: WHYY
URL: https://whyy.org/articles/vineland-data-center-stop-work-orders/
Published: 2026-08-14T14:49:00-04:00

## Relevant excerpt

The city of Vineland, New Jersey, has issued two stop-construction orders to the developers of a data center.
In the orders, the city alleges the 300-megawatt facility began construction work for a liquified natural gas tank, as well as fuel cell units, before receiving approval from the city, according to documents shared with WHYY News.

The project, with the first phase already approved and under construction, is being developed by DataOne for the Nebius Group to support infrastructure for artificial intelligence as part of a $17 billion deal with Microsoft.

DataOne has proposed to use Bloom Energy†www.bloomenergy.com fuel cells, which use a chemical reaction to convert natural gas into electricity, to power the site. The company is also seeking approval for a 1.5 million-gallon on-site storage tank for liquified natural gas, or LNG, in the event access to a natural gas pipeline is interrupted. The city’s planning board must decide whether to approve the proposal after a hearing process.

“Despite DataOne and Nebius launching PR campaigns to say how they are good neighbors and responsible developers, this shows that they have a lack of respect for the Vineland approval process,” said Zac Landicini, who lives about 3 miles from the data center and founded the grassroots group Sustain SJ to advocate against its development. The organization called on city officials to issue the stop-construction orders.
“They don’t really care about the input from the residents because they’re going to do what they want to do, and that’s that. For these corporations that have all this money, they can eat the fines and basically do whatever they want,” Landicini said.

But confusion remains over what exactly has been constructed at the site.

A spokesperson for DataOne said in an email to WHYY News that the city’s notices were “limited to equipment placement work under the approved site plan.”
“We do not expect this temporary pause to affect our construction and delivery timeline,” the spokesperson told WHYY News in an email.

The stop-work order states “you did work without first obtaining a construction permit” and that “we have no prior approvals, plans or permits in reference to Bloom Energy Units.”

It’s unclear why DataOne believed it could start construction on any part of the project without permits.
