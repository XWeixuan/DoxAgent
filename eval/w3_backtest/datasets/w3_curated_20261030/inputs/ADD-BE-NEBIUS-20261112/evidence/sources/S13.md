 Nebius Shares Drop 15% as Infrastructure Delays Hit Revenue Outlook
The AI cloud provider's shares fell approximately 15% in premarket trading after Nebius narrowed its 2026 revenue forecast to the lower end of its previous $3 billion to $3.4 billion range. The company said permitting and power issues at facilities operated by third-party partner DataOne forced the delay of computing capacity for Microsoft, pushing some associated revenue into the first half of 2027. As a result of the infrastructure setbacks, Nebius also raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $27 billion, up from its prior range of $20 billion to $25 billion.
The outlook revision followed a third-quarter revenue of $980.8 million, exceeding the $908 million projected by analysts, with adjusted EBITDA of $465.8 million. Nebius forecasts capital spending of $40 billion to $45 billion in 2027 to support a revenue target of $12 billion to $13 billion. To fund the buildout, the company expects prepayments and financing from investment-grade customers, including Meta and Microsoft, to cover 75% to 85% of its 2027 expenditures. The company ended September with $7.4 billion in cash.
