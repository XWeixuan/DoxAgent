Microsoft (MSFT)

Overview:** Microsoft is a global software and cloud company that provides operating systems, productivity tools like Microsoft 365 and Teams, and its Azure cloud platform to individuals and enterprises. Its high margin, subscription based cloud and enterprise software businesses are a key driver of the strong returns and cash generation that support its solid balance sheet.

Operations:** Microsoft generates about US$138b from Intelligent Cloud, US$140b from Productivity and Business Processes, and US$54b from More Personal Computing, with revenue split roughly between the United States and other countries.

Market Cap:** US$3.8t

Investors looking at the Solid Balance Sheet and Fundamentals theme may find Microsoft hard to ignore, because Azure, Microsoft 365 and Dynamics 365 combine high margin, recurring revenue with a very large cloud backlog and substantial free cash flow. That financial strength is what allows heavy AI and data center spending without stretching the balance sheet, even as investors debate how quickly this investment will show up in margins. At the same time, rising regulatory pressure around cloud licensing and the sheer scale of AI capex keep the story far from risk free. If you want to understand whether the current AI build out and high ROE justify the price, Microsoft rewards a closer look.

Micron Technology (MU)

Overview:** Micron Technology is a global memory and storage manufacturer that supplies DRAM, high bandwidth memory, CXL based memory and data center SSDs that are essential for cloud, AI and enterprise servers, alongside products for PCs, smartphones, cars and industrial equipment. Its cloud memory and core data center units, which focus on high margin DRAM and SSD lines, are the clearest link to the Solid Balance Sheet and Fundamentals theme because they underpin high returns on equity and cash generation, even though mobile and client segments also contribute a significant share of revenue.

Story Continues

Operations:** Micron generates most of its revenue from the Cloud Memory Business Unit at about US$31.3b and the Mobile and Client Business Unit at about US$27.2b, followed by the Core Data Center Business Unit at about US$21.2b and the Automotive and Embedded Business Unit at about US$10.5b, with other activities contributing a small amount.

Market Cap:** US$1.1t

Micron Technology is worth attention if you want exposure to AI infrastructure through a company that already reports very high profitability metrics. The key attraction is its data center memory and storage portfolio, which supports a 50.1% ROE and a 55.9% net margin, helped by high value DRAM, HBM and SSD products that are critical to cloud and AI workloads. Long term customer agreements on DRAM and NAND aim to smooth out some of the classic memory cycle swings. Yet investors still need to weigh peak earnings risk, heavy planned capex and rising competition from Chinese memory producers. If you want to understand whether those strong fundamentals and contracts are enough to offset cycle risk, Micron's story deserves a deeper look.

Palantir Technologies (PLTR)

Overview:** Palantir Technologies builds data and AI platforms that help governments and companies pull together huge volumes of information and turn it into decisions. Its Foundry platform is a key driver of recurring commercial revenue and high customer retention that supports the Solid Balance Sheet and Fundamentals theme. Alongside Foundry, Palantir sells Gotham for defense and intelligence operations, Apollo for software deployment across complex environments, and an AI Platform that connects large language models to customer data.

Operations:** Palantir generates about US$3.2b of revenue from government customers and around US$2.9b from commercial clients worldwide, with the United States contributing roughly US$4.8b.

Market Cap:** US$438.6b

Palantir Technologies combines a mix of high quality fundamentals and punchy expectations that some investors may view as significant. A roughly 31% ROE and net margins near 49% reflect a software model that can generate substantial cash flow, supported by recurring Foundry contracts and long dated government programs such as Maven and the TITAN battlefield system. The balance sheet has no debt and substantial cash, which allows for continued investment in AI while keeping financial risk contained. The trade off is valuation, with the stock priced at a premium, so any setback in growth or margins could affect sentiment quickly. For investors focused on durable earnings and a solid balance sheet, the key question is whether that quality profile and contract pipeline are sufficient to justify the current price level.
