Micron Technology (NASDAQ:MU) shares fell 1.8% in pre-market trading on Tuesday following a report concerning employee incentive payments at the company’s Taiwan operations, while technology stocks also faced broader pressure from higher interest rates.



Taiwan’s Liberty Times reported that Micron plans to introduce its highest incentive payment package to date for employees in Taiwan amid a potential strike involving the company’s labour union.



According to the report, Micron is expected to announce details of the incentive programme in October.



Workers seek changes to bonus structure



Micron’s labour union is seeking changes to the company’s existing performance bonus system, including a more transparent profit-sharing mechanism linked to company profitability, according to the report.



Liberty Times indicated that Micron intends to continue communicating with employees about the matter.



The reported potential strike and proposed changes to employee compensation remain subject to ongoing discussions. The supplied information does not indicate that a strike has begun or that the terms of a revised incentive programme have been finalised.



Taiwan remains an important manufacturing location



Taiwan is a significant manufacturing location for Micron’s operations in the Asia-Pacific region, increasing investor attention on developments involving the company’s workforce there.



The report comes as technology stocks face broader market pressure associated with higher interest rates and bond yields, which can affect equity valuations across the sector.



Micron’s 1.8% pre-market decline coincided with both the Taiwan labour report and wider weakness among technology shares. However, the supplied information does not establish the extent to which either factor caused the company’s share-price movement.
